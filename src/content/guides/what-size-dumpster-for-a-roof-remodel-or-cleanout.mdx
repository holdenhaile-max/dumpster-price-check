The table lists every rental these companies publish. Size, days, included tons, and price are on the row. Checked is the day that page was read, and the source link is the company page. Call for quote means the page named a size and did not name a dollar price. This page does not guess how many squares of shingles fit, how many rooms fit, or how many tons a cleanout weighs.

## Cleanout

Bin There Dump That Aggieland describes its bins on the rates page. That page says to contact them for a quote. No dollar price is printed.

- 4 yard: small home cleanout, and the page says the bin is useful for small driveways. 10 ft long, 5.5 ft wide, 2 ft high. A typical rental is 7 days.
- 6 yard: a bit more than a small cleanout. The page lists roof shingles as a material this bin can hold. That line is an allowed material, not a roof size. 10 ft long, 5.5 ft wide, 3 ft high.
- 10 yard: small to medium cleanout, 4 pickup truck loads. 10 ft long, 7 ft wide, 4 ft high.
- 15 yard: medium cleanup. 11 ft long, 8 ft wide, 4.5 ft high.
- 20 yard: medium to large jobs, up to 8 pickup truck loads. 11 ft long, 8 ft wide, 6 ft high.

BVR Waste and Recycling's residential page lists three sizes and no dollar price.

- 14 yard: 6 pickup loads. Home projects, junk removal, and interior or exterior renovations. The page describes it as a fit for small home clean-up projects.
- 20 yard: 8 pickup loads. Heavy-debris home clean-outs and remodeling cleanup.
- 30 yard: 14 pickup loads. Office or large home renovation projects, or junk removal.

The commercial page lists a 40-yard at 16 pickup loads for large construction and remodeling projects. It also lists 20-yard and 30-yard with the same pickup-load lines as the residential page. The commercial FAQ says rental periods run from a few days to several months. It does not name one included day count or a dollar price.

Pronto Services says a home cleanout is a reason to rent. It points to the 30-yard and 40-yard for estate cleanouts. The 20-yard holds approximately 6 pickup truck loads and fits most residential driveways. Weight limits on that page are 8,000 lbs (4 tons), 10,000 lbs (5 tons), and 12,000 lbs (6 tons). No dollar price is published. Rentals run 7 to 10 days, and the daily extension price is not published.

BCS Junk Removal lists a 15-yard dumpster at $380 for 3 days and $420 for 7 days. The dump fee is $60 per ton. Included tons are not listed. The company says the box fits in one parking space. The page does not call that box a cleanout size.

## Remodel

CTI Response prints a project on each size. It does not print a dollar price, included days, or included tons.

- 10 yard: small remodels, cleanouts, and heavy material.
- 20 yard: renovations, roofing, and mixed debris.
- 30 yard: new construction and multi-trade sites.
- 40 yard: large builds and demolition.

The FAQ says a 10 or 20 yard box is usually a good fit for remodels and cleanouts.

Texas Disposal Systems ties a project to each size and says the price is customized.

- 20 yard: single-room renovations, flooring removal, deck demolition, and moderate landscaping. The FAQ says a 20-yard is sufficient for most single-room renovations. Dimensions on the page: 22L x 8W x 3.5H. The page does not name the unit. It holds 5,000-8,000 lbs.
- 30 yard: multi-room renovations, roof tear-offs, and mid-size construction and demolition. The FAQ recommends a 30-yard for a multi-room remodel, a kitchen gut, or a full home renovation. Dimensions: 22L x 8W x 6H. It holds 8,000-12,000 lbs.
- 40 yard: commercial construction, full home demolitions, storm cleanups, and major renovations. Dimensions: 22L x 8W x 8H. It holds 12,000-16,000 lbs.

No dollar price, included day count, or per-ton fee is published. The page says a weight allowance is included and that an overweight load can add a charge. It does not publish the ton allowance or the charge.

Pronto Services names a kitchen remodel as a home project and says the 30-yard and 40-yard are for whole-home renovations. It does not publish a dollar price.

BVR's 20-yard line says remodeling cleanup. The 30-yard line says a large home renovation. No dollar price is published.

The priced rows are not labeled as remodel prices by those companies. Waste Falcon's 10-yard booking page is $365. That page lists the box as 12 ft long, 3 ft high, and 6.5 ft wide, and it does not list days or tons. The 20-yard booking page is $425, up to 7 days, 0 tons included, then $42 per ton, and $8 a day after day 10. The box is 14 ft long, 8 ft wide, and 6 ft high. The marketing page says $45 per ton and $8 a day after day 7. This page uses the booking page. Aggieland Dumpster Rentals does not state an exact yard size. The FAQ says the trailers average just under 20 yards. Prices are $300, $350, $400, and $450 plus tax for 1, 2, 3, and 7 days, with 2 tons included.

## Roof

Two pages name roofing as a use for one size. Neither publishes a price, a square count, or a ton weight for a roof.

CTI Response lists the 20-yard for renovations, roofing, and mixed debris. Texas Disposal Systems lists roof tear-offs on the 30-yard. The same Texas Disposal Systems page lists roofing projects as a type of job it takes, and it does not give that job a second size.

Bin There Dump That Aggieland lists roof shingles as a material for the 6-yard and the 10-yard. Pronto Services lists roofing shingles as accepted and does not tie them to one yard size. An allowed material is not a recommended size. If the page does not name the yard size for a roof, this page leaves it blank.

Waste Falcon's homepage FAQ lists roofing materials as allowed. No Waste Falcon booking page states a yard size for a roof. The 10-yard, 20-yard, and 30-yard prices above are the booking-page prices, not a roof price.
